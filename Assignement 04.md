**GENERAL CATALOG OF ARTIFICIAL SPACE OBJECTS (GCAT)**

*McDowell, Jonathan C., 2020. General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects, Release 1.8.8 , https://planet4589.org/space/gcat*

1. What is the dataset?
This dataset documents artificial space objects, including satellites, rockets stage, components and debris.

2. Where does it come from? Who made it?
The dataset is compiled by Jonathan C. McDowell, an astrophysicist and space sustainability analyst. He also studies the history of spaceflight and has published Jonathan’s Space Report, a newsletter documenting satellite launches and space activities, since 1989.

3. Why? What was its original purpose?
It's purpose is to build a catalog built to be a historical record, held to a standard of documented provenance rather than operational convenience

4. What makes it interesting?
It is one continuous record, from 1942 to today. The launch table opens with the V-2 and runs to launches from the past few days. There is no break in methodology at the end of the Cold War, no change of custodian, no segment where coverage silently thins because a contract lapsed. Beyond the dataset itself, the website contains a wealth of additional data and detailed explanations that help readers understand how satellites, orbits, and the broader spaceflight system work.

5. What are some issues you anticipate using it? Where does it fall short?
The dataset documents space objects but does not provide economic information, such as the costs of building and launching satellites or their enviromental impact. This is not a limitation of the dataset itself, given its historical purpose, but it matters for my research. My focus is on satellites in low Earth orbit (LEO) that provide internet connectivity, and I want to connect their presence in orbit to the question of “who holds power.” To explore this, I would need additional sources on corporate ownership, funding, and control over satellite internet infrastructure.
